# UX/Design Thought Leaders on AI: Who Believes What

A reference of public positions taken by major UX/design voices on AI's role in UX practice, compiled to give a custom UX-AI course generator the actual landscape of views (not just one perspective). Each entry includes the leader's current role, a 2-4 sentence stance summary, 2-3 primary sources, and a tag.

Tag legend: **optimist**, **skeptic**, **pragmatist**, **craft-focused**, **displacement-worried**, **infrastructure-builder**, **labor-advocate**, **researcher-of-the-medium**.

---

## Don Norman
**Role:** Co-founder, Nielsen Norman Group; author of *The Design of Everyday Things* and *Design for a Better World*; founder of UC San Diego Design Lab.
**Stance:** Norman frames AI as a powerful but undisciplined material that must be subordinated to "humanity-centered design," his expansion of human-centered design to include ecosystems, externalities and sustainability. He repeatedly warns about black-box AI eroding trust, insists AI should augment rather than replace human capability, and argues we should retire the term "artificial intelligence" itself because it misleads designers about what the tech is doing. He sees autonomous systems, AI and AR as exciting *problems UX must solve* rather than gifts to celebrate.
**Tag:** pragmatist / skeptic of hype, optimist about UX's role
**Sources:**
- [Rethinking the lifecycle of design in the face of AI with Don Norman — UX Magazine podcast (2024)](https://uxmag.com/podcast/don-norman)
- [Navigating the Future of Design: Don Norman on UX, AI, and Design Leadership — YouTube (July 28, 2025)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z0xfLHWAEIo)
- [Embracing humanity-centered design: insights from Don Norman — UserTesting podcast](https://www.usertesting.com/resources/podcast/what-is-humanity-centered-design)

## Jakob Nielsen
**Role:** Co-founder, Nielsen Norman Group (semi-retired from NN/g operations); writes the Substack *Jakob Nielsen on UX*.
**Stance:** The most outspoken AI optimist among the old guard. Nielsen argues that 2026 is the inflection year for **Generative UI** — software that draws itself per-user, per-context — and that static interfaces are becoming obsolete. He preaches urgency ("You won't lose your job to AI, but to someone who uses AI better than you do"), claims accessibility has "failed" and generative UI is the answer, and tells UX professionals to start small (personas, microcopy) and scale up. His position has *strengthened* over 2023-2026, from cautious in early ChatGPT days to evangelistic.
**Tag:** optimist / displacement-worried-for-laggards
**Sources:**
- [Generative UI from Gemini 3 Pro — jakobnielsenphd.substack.com](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/generative-ui-google)
- [UX Needs a Sense of Urgency About AI](https://jakobnielsenphd.substack.com/p/ux-needs-a-sense-of-urgency-about)

## Brad Frost
**Role:** Founder, Brad Frost Web; design systems consultant; author of *Atomic Design*.
**Stance:** Treats AI as a "smart-but-sometimes-unsophisticated junior developer" whose output must be reviewed. His big argument is that **design systems are the rails AI needs**: structured tokens, components and organizational context keep generated UI on-brand and accessible. He launched a paid course (with TJ Pitre and Ian Frost) on AI + Design Systems and is now writing about "agentic design systems." Underneath the pragmatism he's openly grieving the de-humanizing effects of AI on team craft and collaboration.
**Tag:** pragmatist / craft-focused / infrastructure-builder
**Sources:**
- [AI and Design Systems — bradfrost.com](https://bradfrost.com/blog/post/ai-and-design-systems/)
- [Design systems in the time of AI](https://bradfrost.com/blog/post/design-systems-in-the-time-of-ai/)

## Josh Clark
**Role:** Founder, Big Medium; co-author with Veronika Kindred of *Sentient Design: AI and the New Digital Experience* (Rosenfeld, 2024).
**Stance:** Clark popularized **"Sentient Design"** — AI as a *design material* (alongside pixels and code) for interfaces that feel almost self-aware in responding to users. He pushes designers to stop fearing AI and start using it as the default substrate for adaptive, intelligent UX, anchored by what he calls "casual intelligence" (smart defaults, predictive keyboards, recommendations) and "sensible defaults" patterns. He is one of the most concrete voices on *what* AI-native UX patterns look like in practice.
**Tag:** optimist / craft-focused
**Sources:**
- [Sentient Design: AI and the Next Chapter of UX — Big Medium talk page](https://bigmedium.com/speaking/sentient-design-josh-clark-talk.html)
- [AMA: Josh Clark on AI and design systems — Design Better podcast](https://designbetterpodcast.com/p/ama-josh-clark-on-ai-and-design-systems)

## Jared Spool
**Role:** Co-CEO, Center Centre; founder of UIE; runs the Maven course *UX & Design for Today's AI Functionality* and the Smashing masterclass *The Modern UX Practitioner*.
**Stance:** Argues that AI doesn't change UX *fundamentals* but raises the strategic stakes: UX must lead how AI is infused into products, "design for ambiguity, adaptivity and emotion," and respect AI's limits. He frames AI as a productivity booster (blacksmith analogy — professions evolve, they don't vanish overnight), predicts an "AI-fueled UX resurgence," and is one of the loudest voices saying Nielsen-style heuristics weren't built for systems that talk back.
**Tag:** pragmatist / optimist-about-UX-role
**Sources:**
- [UX & Design for Today's AI Functionality — Maven](https://maven.com/centercentre/uxai)
- [Extended Q&A with Jared Spool: How AI Will Bring on a UX Resurgence — Center Centre community](https://leaders.centercentre.com/posts/extended-qa-15-min-with-jared-spool-how-ai-will-bring-on-a-ux-resurgence)

## Kate Moran
**Role:** Senior Vice President, Nielsen Norman Group.
**Stance:** The most empirical voice on AI in UX research. Moran's published work shows ChatGPT-4 hitting an 80% error rate on UX audits, surveys being contaminated by participants pasting AI answers, and broad AI features being harder for users to adopt than narrow ones. She argues the first wave of AI UX tools were marketing-driven and that "narrow AI" (Spotify playlist generator) is more usable than broad AI (ChatGPT). She is cautiously optimistic about a second, more thoughtful generation of tools.
**Tag:** pragmatist / researcher-of-the-medium
**Sources:**
- [Kate Moran about UX Leadership and AI in UX Research — UXtweak](https://blog.uxtweak.com/kate-moran-about-ux-leadership/)
- [Testing ChatGPT-4 for UX Audits: 80% error rate — LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kate-m-moran_testing-chatgpt-4-for-ux-audits-shows-an-activity-7120806830829654016-vNTa)

## Sarah Drasner
**Role:** Senior Director of Engineering at Google (Web, Android, iOS, observability and Multiplatform Core Infrastructure); previously VP DX at Netlify.
**Stance:** Comes at AI from the DX/engineering-leadership side rather than UX-craft. Her public posture is operationally pragmatic: AI as a leverage tool for engineering teams, with strong emphasis on the *management* skills (career planning, the human work that doesn't disappear) that scale through AI-assisted teams. She is not a prominent commentator on whether AI threatens design itself; her substantive AI work shows up in how she's building DX platforms.
**Tag:** pragmatist / infrastructure-builder

## Vitaly Friedman
**Role:** Founder and editor-in-chief, Smashing Magazine; senior UX consultant for the European Parliament.
**Stance:** Has shifted hard toward AI-pattern *practice*. Runs the workshops "Design Patterns for AI Interfaces" and "Why You Will Not Be Replaced by AI," and publishes pattern libraries covering daemons, clustering, style lenses, structured presets and agentic UX. His core critique: most AI products today feel "bolted on, disconnected, generic, slow, inaccurate, wasteful," and the answer is better UX *patterns*, not better models. Strongly pragmatic optimist about designers' role.
**Tag:** pragmatist / craft-focused / optimist
**Sources:**

## Aarron Walter
**Role:** Independent consultant; co-host of *Design Better* podcast (with Eli Woolery); former VP of Design Education at InVision; author of *Designing for Emotion*.
**Stance:** Balanced and increasingly worried. He has called AI a force turning designers into "unicorns" suddenly fluent across disciplines, but in 2024-2026 he's been documenting "cracks" in the creative process — silos deepening, automation replacing collaboration, design leaders at Microsoft, CVS, Meta, Salesforce and JPMorgan voicing concern. His Design Better episodes explicitly treat "the uncertainty of AI" as a topic to sit with, not resolve.
**Tag:** pragmatist drifting toward displacement-worried (notable shift 2023 → 2026)
**Sources:**

## Daniel Burka
**Role:** Director of Product & Design, Resolve to Save Lives (Simple project, used in hospitals across India, Bangladesh, Ethiopia for 5M+ hypertension/diabetes patients); former Google Ventures design partner.
**Stance:** Burka has very little public AI-evangelism. His public talks (e.g., "Design actually can save lives… but not by itself" at TalkUX 2025) keep emphasizing context, constraints and humility in low-resource healthcare settings, where mainstream AI tooling is a poor fit. His implicit position: AI is a marginal tool for global-health UX where reliability, low bandwidth and trained healthcare workers dominate the problem.

**Role:** Co-founder and CEO, Sundial (AI-powered data analysis); former VP Product Design, Facebook; author *The Making of a Manager*; writes *The Looking Glass* on Substack.
**Stance:** Has effectively *become* an AI founder. Her recent public framing is "from managing people to managing AI" — AI isn't a designer's threat so much as a new kind of report, and the leadership skill set (clear briefs, taste, evaluation) maps directly onto agent workflows. Strong pro-AI tone, focused on data democratization and helping non-analysts make better product decisions.

## Soleio Cuervo
**Role:** Co-founder and Managing Partner, Combine VC; early designer at Facebook (Like button) and Dropbox; design-driven investor in Figma, Vercel, Replit, Perplexity, Vanta.
**Stance:** Tells designers not to think of AI as replacing their job but as *increasing their surface area of impact*. Frames the next shift as **orchestration** — less pushing pixels, more guiding intelligent systems. Argues tools now allow tiny teams to ship work with more range and coherence than ever; tomorrow's great designers may come from outside traditional design. Optimist with a craft sensibility, speaking primarily from an investor's vantage.

## Tobias van Schneider
**Role:** Co-founder, Mymind; publisher of *The DESK Magazine*; former Spotify designer.
**Stance:** Self-described "romantic" pro-tech designer who refuses to outsource his core craft to AI ("I am really good at those tasks; using AI would diminish the work"). Notes his product Mymind was AI-first since 2019 — but always *quiet, background, human-feeling* AI. Holds the line that integrity and soul are non-negotiable, while acknowledging AI will be part of the toolset. One of the clearest "craft-first, AI-second" voices.

## Mike Monteiro

## Erika Hall
**Role:** Co-founder, Mule Design; author of *Just Enough Research* and *Conversational Design*.
**Stance:** Sharp, evidence-based skeptic. Tested ChatGPT for songwriting prompts and found the output "laughably awful." In 2025 interviews she has said "everybody at the top of these organizations is lying — we're in a complete fog," and cites Stanford/MIT/Oxford research on AI "work slop" — mediocre AI output that creates the illusion of productivity. Her deeper point: organizations have forgotten how to talk to each other and to users, and AI is masking that rot.
**Tag:** skeptic
**Sources:**
- [Talk to Each Other: Erika Hall on Why Human Conversation Is Still the Most Powerful Design Tool — Bootcamp (Mar 2026)](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/talk-to-each-other-erika-hall-on-why-human-conversation-is-still-the-most-powerful-design-tool-30b55663aeb1)

## Ethan Marcotte
**Role:** Independent designer/writer; coined "responsive web design"; author of *You Deserve a Tech Union*; former 18F.
**Stance:** The clearest "deskilling and labor" voice in design. Frames generative AI as an *existential labor problem*, citing training-data theft and worrying that reliance on these systems erodes the value of skilled labor. Cites Hollywood unions' approach as the template for tech workers. His stance has *hardened* visibly across 2023-2025, from cautious to actively advocating organized resistance.
**Tag:** skeptic / labor-advocate / displacement-worried (notable hardening 2023 → 2025)
**Sources:**

## Karri Saarinen
**Role:** Co-founder and CEO, Linear; previously principal designer at Airbnb and Coinbase.
**Stance:** AI-era craft evangelist. His widely-quoted line: "Mass production separated the maker's hand from the object; AI attempts to separate the maker's *judgment and taste* from creation itself." Argues craft can never be fully outsourced and that "design is search, not a production pipeline." In 2026 declared "issue tracking is dead" and pivoted Linear toward AI-native work coordination, so he's putting product money where his mouth is on the AI-era product shape.
**Tag:** craft-focused / pragmatist / infrastructure-builder

## Maggie Appleton
**Role:** Designer at Elicit (AI research-assistant lab); writer/illustrator at maggieappleton.com.

## Andy Budd
**Role:** Venture Partner, Seedcamp; design coach; founder of Clearleft, Leading Design and UX London.
**Stance:** The "makers to curators" thesis: as craft becomes commoditized by AI, designers shift to **editing, curating and selecting** from AI-generated options rather than building from scratch. Advocates for "AI-literate, full-stack designers" who work fluently from UX through code. ## Daniel Eden
**Role:** Product Designer, Meta (currently lead designer on Meta Horizon profile, mobile and VR, 2025+); previously Figma, Facebook, Dropbox.
**Stance:** Articulates an **"Agentic Experience Design"** framework with three layers: what agents can *do*, how humans stay *in control*, and how agents *talk to each other*.
